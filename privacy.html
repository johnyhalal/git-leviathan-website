Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 September 2026

GitLeviathan is a desktop application for working with Git repositories. It runs entirely on your own computer. This policy explains what data the app and this website handle, and what they do not.

The short version

GitLeviathan does not collect your personal information. Your repositories, credentials, file names, and commit messages stay on your machine and go only to the Git hosting services you choose to connect to. The app does send a small amount of anonymous usage analytics to help us understand how it is used — this contains no personal data and no information about your repositories, and you can turn it off at any time. Details are below.

Data the app stores locally

To function, GitLeviathan keeps the following on your device, using your operating system's standard storage locations:

  • Application settings — such as your theme preference and window state.
  • Account credentials and access tokens for the Git services you connect (for example GitHub or GitLab). Where the platform supports it, these are held in your operating system's secure credential store.
  • Local clones of your repositories and their Git history, in the folders you choose.

This data never leaves your device except when you ask the app to communicate with a Git host (for example, to clone, fetch, pull, or push).

Anonymous usage analytics

To understand how GitLeviathan is used and where to improve it, the app sends anonymous usage analytics to Aptabase, a privacy-focused analytics service. This is enabled by default; the first time it runs, the app shows a notice, and you can turn it off at any time from the app's General settings.

When enabled, the app records a few coarse events — when the app is opened, when a commit is created, and when it checks for updates. Each event is sent with only:

  • a random anonymous identifier generated on your device, which is not linked to you and contains no personal information;
  • coarse technical properties — your operating system and its version, the app version, and your language/locale.

We do not send, and Aptabase does not receive, any repository paths, file names, commit messages, branch names, account details, IP-based location, or any other personal or repository content. Aptabase's handling of the data it processes on our behalf is described in its privacy policy. If you would prefer to send nothing at all, simply disable usage analytics in the app's settings.

Third-party services you connect

When you sign in to a Git hosting provider or clone from a Git URL, GitLeviathan communicates directly with that service on your behalf. Your use of those services is governed by their own privacy policies, not ours:

  • GitHub Privacy Statement
  • GitLab Privacy Statement

We do not sit between you and these providers, and we do not receive a copy of anything you send to or from them.

Downloads and updates

Application downloads and release files are hosted on GitHub. When you download GitLeviathan or check for a new version, your request goes to GitHub, whose data handling is covered by the GitHub privacy statement linked above.

This website

This website is a static download page. It loads Google Tag Manager, which we use to measure basic site usage — for example, which download button visitors click. Google Tag Manager, and any tags it loads, may set cookies and process data such as your IP address and browser information; this is handled by Google under its privacy policy. We use this only in aggregate to understand site traffic and downloads, and never to identify you personally.

Separately, depending on how the site is hosted, the hosting provider may keep standard server access logs (such as IP addresses and request times) for security and operational purposes.

Children's privacy

GitLeviathan is a developer tool and is not directed at children under the age of 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. When we do, we will revise the “Last updated” date at the top of this page.

Contact

If you have questions about this policy, please open an issue on the GitLeviathan GitHub repository.
